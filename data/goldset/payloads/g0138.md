### Why this works

* **`max_ending`** holds the *largest* product of a sub‑array that **must** end at the current element `n`.  
* **`min_ending`** holds the *smallest* (most negative) product of a sub‑array that ends at `n`.

* At each step we consider three possibilities for both trackers:
  1. Start a new sub‑array at the current element (`n` alone).
  2. Extend the previous best sub‑array (`prev_max * n`).
  3. Extend the previous worst sub‑array (`prev_min * n`).

* `max_so_far` records the best product we have ever seen, regardless of where the sub‑array ends.

The algorithm runs in **O(n)** time and **O(1)** extra space.
